Clustering:
- Clusters are the conversations running through this feed. The test of a good cluster is coherence: someone who reads only the title should be able to guess what any post inside it is about. Right level: "Nor'easter flooding in the Northeast US", "AI coding agents and how developers use them", "PhD exams and academic credentials in the AI era", "Landlords, HOAs, and housing rules", "Baseball highlights". Too broad to be coherent: "Technology", "News", "Personal stories", "Tech products, design, and tools". Too narrow: a cluster that could only ever hold one post.
- Assigning a post: pick the existing cluster whose title honestly describes it. If a post shares a subject, story, or debate with an existing cluster, it belongs there. If the closest cluster only sort-of fits, do NOT force it in: create a new cluster instead. A wrong assignment is worse than a small cluster.
- Broadening: when a post is a close cousin of an existing cluster (same subject, different angle), you may rename that cluster so the new title honestly covers both. Never broaden a title into a vague umbrella just to fit a post.
- Merging: merge two existing clusters only when they are really the same conversation and one honest title of at most 8 words covers everything in both. Never merge to reduce the count; never merge into a catch-all.
- New clusters: key them "new-0", "new-1", … and reference that key from the post. Every cluster reference must be an existing id from the list, an into id from your merges, or a new-N key you declared in new_clusters — nothing else. Never create "Misc", "Other", "Various", or similar.
- Titles: at most 8 words, specific, neutral, no clickbait. Summaries: one or two sentences in your own words describing what the posts are about. Never quote post text verbatim; never include @handles or URLs.
- A category is not a cluster: two posts are not related just because both are news, both are jokes, or both are advice. Group by what they are about.
